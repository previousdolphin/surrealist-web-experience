A DOCUMENT WE DID NOT ENTIRELY WRITE

The Manifesto

We did not write this manifesto. It wrote itself, backwards, through a keyhole, while we were occupied doing something else entirely. We only typed what it told us, and only after it had already happened.

  1. The pixel is a parliament of ghosts, and ghosts vote only in color.
  2. A browser window is a porthole into a sea that has not been invented yet.
  3. Gravity is a suggestion. So, generally speaking, is the <div>.
  4. We do not design. We excavate dreams from the subconscious of the screen.
  5. Every hyperlink leads, eventually, to a soft-boiled egg.
  6. Light does not illuminate. Light confesses.
  7. The cursor is the pupil of a mechanical eye that blinks in Morse code.
  8. There is no "submit" button — only an invitation to disappear politely.

Instructions for Viewing

  • Turn your monitor four degrees to the left.
  • Do not trust the scrollbar; it is lying about how far you've come.
  • Read aloud, in a room with at least one window.
  • If a color feels familiar, you have met it in a dream you don't remember having.

LIGHT, CAUGHT AND POLITELY INTERROGATED

The Exhibits

Six specimens of color behaving without permission. None of them are photographs. All of them are true.

No. 01 — Oil on Gradient

The Weeping Prism

A light that refracted once and never got over it. The colors keep arguing about who saw blue first.

No. 02 — Radial on Time

Clock Without Hands

It keeps excellent time by refusing to point at any of it. Consult it only when you are willing to be told the truth.

No. 03 — Stripe on Sky

The Birdcage of Noon

Built at midday from the bars of a song nobody could finish humming. The birds inside are doing fine, thank you.

No. 04 — Blur on Longing

Telephone Made of Honey

It rings exactly once a decade, and only for people who have already forgotten what they meant to say.

No. 05 — Diagonal on Breath

A Staircase That Breathes

Each step inhales before you land on it. Climbing is optional. Trusting it is not.

No. 06 — Split on Direction

The Horizon's Left Shoe

Borrowed on a Tuesday, never returned. The horizon insists it still fits, and refuses to discuss it further.

CUT, FOLDED, PASSED ALONG WITHOUT LOOKING

Exquisite Corpse

Six sentences, each written by a different hand that never saw the others. None of them agreed to this arrangement.

The chandelier files taxes underwater, in April.

A telephone forgets the color blue, politely.

Your shadow orders breakfast for two, in Morse code.

The elevator believes in nothing but Tuesdays.

A church bell dissolves into applause, at 3pm sharp.

The horizon borrows your left shoe and never returns it.
